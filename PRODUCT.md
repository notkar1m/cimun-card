# Product

## Register

product

## Users

JCIMUN delegates and CIMUN board members — attendees and organizers of the CIMUN (Cairo International Model United Nations) conference and its junior edition. They use the app primarily on their phones, in short bursts between conference sessions or while standing at a partner restaurant/cafe counter, to look up an offer and redeem it on the spot. Speed and clarity in the moment of redemption matter more than long browsing sessions.

Two other roles exist (vendor staff at partner businesses, and a superadmin who manages offers/accounts). They are internal/operational users, not the primary design focus, but their screens should still read as a modern, considered product rather than a stock admin scaffold — see Anti-references.

The product is scoped to Cimun Card and its deals only: browsing/redeeming offers (delegate side) and managing offers/vendors/users (operational side). No other features (e.g. event check-in/QR scanning) are in scope.

## Product Purpose

Cimun Card gives conference delegates and board members access to exclusive discounts at restaurants and cafes near the conference venue. A user browses available offers, taps into one, and redeems it with a single tap. Redeeming immediately shows a branded "membership card" moment on screen that the delegate flashes to staff in person — no code to read aloud, no typing, just a visual check. Success looks like: a delegate can go from "hungry, has 2 minutes between sessions" to "showing staff a card" in under 10 seconds, and the card moment itself feels like a perk worth having, not a coupon.

## Brand Personality

Premium, polished, trustworthy. This should feel like carrying a real membership/rewards card, not a discount-app coupon. Confident, a little upscale, understated rather than loud — the kind of thing a delegate feels good pulling out at a counter in front of other people. Trustworthy matters specifically because staff and the delegate both need to feel the "card" moment is legitimate at a glance.

## Anti-references

- Not a generic coupon-clipping app (Groupon-style badges, starburst "50% OFF" stickers, countdown-timer urgency).
- Not a bare-bones, unstyled admin scaffold for the vendor/superadmin portals — those are still allowed to look like dashboards (tables, forms, a nav rail, density), but the dashboard should feel deliberately designed: a considered type scale, real spacing rhythm, a coherent component system, not default-browser-form-element energy. The end-user surface stays the more premium, consumer-product treatment; the operational portals are calmer and denser but equally polished.
- Not loud/gamified with confetti, badges, or streak mechanics — this isn't a rewards-points game, it's a simple membership benefit.
- Avoid anything that reads as "generic SaaS product" (soft blue-on-white cards, gradient-text headings, hero-metric stat blocks) — this is a phone-first consumer moment, not a B2B dashboard.

## Design Principles

- The redeem moment is the product. Every other screen (feed, offer detail) exists to get the delegate to that single tap and the card that follows; design backward from that moment.
- Feels premium through restraint, not decoration. Confidence comes from typography, spacing, and one well-chosen accent, not gradients or badges.
- One thumb, one hand. Every primary action must be reachable and tappable without adjusting grip, because delegates are often holding a plate, a badge, or a friend's arm while doing this.
- Legible at a glance, in a crowd, on a bright or dim screen. Contrast and size have to survive a rushed café counter interaction, not just a design review.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: minimum 4.5:1 text contrast, 44x44px minimum tap targets, visible focus states, no color-only status indicators.
